## 1. The editing identity: "Bruh Cinema"

**One-line style:** *Tamil-movie treatment of everyday bruh moments: committed cinematic drama that breaks into comedy.*

The identity is a **repeatable kit** of 6 signatures. Use at least **3 per Reel** so the style is recognisable, without becoming a rigid template.

| # | Signature | What it is | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **Character name card** | Freeze frame plus a bold title card naming the "character", Tamil mass-movie intro style. Examples: **"<RABBIT'S NAME> 🐇 — The Villain"**, **"R15 — Mass Hero"**, **"ME — The Struggling Hero"** | The classic Tamil cinema intro trope. Funny, recognisable, works on every pillar |
| **S2** | **The BGM switch** | Epic or emotional music → hard stop or record scratch → comedy track or tragedy at the bruh moment | Your core joke mechanic ([15 §2.2](15-tamil-music-and-audio-strategy.md#22-the-bgm-switch-expectation--reality)) |
| **S3** | **"Bruh" sting** | One recurring short sound plus a visual hit (quick zoom, shake, brief desaturation) at every fail or awkward moment | A sonic logo. Builds a running joke |
| **S4** | **Episode tag** | Small consistent corner label: `BRUH MOMENT #07`, `STUPID PURCHASE #03`, `PET CAST` | Series recognition. Helps follow conversion ([14 §8](14-creator-identity-and-content-pillars.md#8-series-recurring-formats-that-give-people-a-reason-to-follow)) |
| **S5** | **Two-tone grade** | A "movie mode" grade (warm/teal contrast, slight grain) for the dramatic part → a flat, natural "reality" grade after the switch | The visual version of the BGM switch |
| **S6** | **Tanglish captions** | Consistent caption style: bold sans-serif, white with black stroke, a yellow highlight on the punch word, written how you speak | Personality, plus readable for non-Tamil viewers |

### Brand kit spec (build in Phase 3)

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Title / name-card font** | A heavy condensed display font (for example *Bebas Neue* or *Anton*, free from Google Fonts), all caps, the classic movie-poster feel |
| **Caption font** | *Montserrat ExtraBold* or *Poppins Bold* (Google Fonts, commercial-OK) |
| **Colours** | Movie-mode accent **#FFC400** (mass yellow) for name cards · white captions with black stroke · fail-red **#FF3B30** for "BRUH" stamps |
| **Name-card layout** | Freeze frame, slight zoom (105%), darken the background 30%, title slides in from the left in 6 frames, subtitle (role) fades in below, with a hit SFX on the title |
| **"Bruh" sting** | One short custom sound (record yourself saying "bruh", or a distinctive SFX) plus 4-frame zoom punch plus 2-frame shake |
| **Episode tag** | Top-left *inside the safe zone* (below the top 250 px UI), small, 70% opacity |
| **Grades** | 2 presets: `MovieMode` (warm highlights, teal shadows, +contrast, 5% grain) and `Reality` (neutral, slightly flat) |
| **Cover template** | 3:4-safe ([07 §6](07-instagram-seo-and-optimization.md#6-covers--thumbnails)): character or freeze frame plus a 3–5 word title in the display font, plus the series tag |

## 2. Shooting style by pillar

| Pillar | Signature shots | Camera notes |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 Bruh Moments** | Static wide for the skit, punch-in close-up for the reaction, POV shots | Tripod at eye level. Shoot the reaction separately for a clean close-up |
| **P2 Pet Cast** | Ground-level eye-line shots of the rabbits and birds, macro details (nose, feathers), slow-mo entries | Get *down to their level*. 60/120 fps for "hero" moments. **No flash, no loud noises**, patience ([14 §9](14-creator-identity-and-content-pillars.md#9-responsibility-guardrails-protect-the-account)) |
| **P3 Builds & Gadgets** | Top-down process, timelapse, macro detail, before/after match-framed | Lock the tripod position for before/after. Even, soft light |
| **P4 Roads** | Low-angle hero shot of the R15 or Verna, rolling shots from a passenger, helmet or chest-mount POV, dashboard POV, golden-hour silhouettes | **Never handheld while riding or driving.** Mounts only. 4K for reframing, 60 fps for ramps |

### Low-budget "cinematic" tricks (phone)
- **Low hero angle:** phone near the ground, tilted up, slow push-in → instant "mass" feel
- **Foreground framing:** shoot through a doorway, past plants or the bike handle for depth
- **Golden hour:** shoot vehicles and outdoor scenes in the first or last hour of sunlight
- **Slow-mo entries:** 120 fps for the dramatic beat, real speed for the comedy reality
- **Practical lights:** room LEDs or the desk lamp as moody backlight for "villain" scenes

## 3. Reel format library

Each format: **concept → structure → audio → signatures → example from your life.** Lengths follow [05 §6](05-hooks-and-storytelling.md#6-ideal-length-by-format).

### F1 · Mass Entry for Mundane Things *(P1/P2/P4 · 8–15 s)*
- **Structure:** cold-open slow-mo → name card freeze (S1) → mass BGM peak → reality reveal (S2) → bruh sting (S3)
- **Audio:** mass hero BGM → comedy track or silence
- **Example:** *"R15 — Mass Hero"* name card, slow-mo walk to the bike… it doesn't start. Record scratch. Pushing it down the street.

### F2 · Expectation vs Reality (BGM Switch) *(P1/P3 · 10–20 s)*
- **Structure:** text hook "What I planned 😎" → dramatic build → switch → "What happened 🤦"
- **Audio:** romantic or epic → tragedy or comedy
- **Example:** DIY desk-shelf plan with the "training montage" BGM → shelf collapses → sad violin.

### F3 · Dialogue Dub *(P1/P2 · 7–20 s)*
- **Structure:** setup text (the situation) → famous Tamil dialogue, lip-synced or mapped onto a pet or object → reaction close-up
- **Audio:** comedy or movie dialogue from the library
- **Example:** Text: *"Me asking the rabbit why the charger cable is bitten"* → villain dialogue as the rabbit stares back.

### F4 · The Pet Cast Episode *(P2 · 10–25 s)*
- **Structure:** character intro (S1, first appearance per series) → mini "plot" (stealing food, ignoring you, bird gossip) → twist ending
- **Audio:** character theme ([15 §2.4](15-tamil-music-and-audio-strategy.md#24-genre-casting-music-as-character))
- **Example:** *"Episode 4: The Great Carrot Heist"*, a thriller BGM edit of the rabbit stealing a carrot.

### F5 · Stupid Purchase #N *(P1/P3 · 15–30 s)*
- **Structure:** hook "I bought this for ₹___. Why? 🤪" → mass-intro unboxing (S1) → real test → **verdict card**: "WORTH IT 💸" / "BRUH 🤦"
- **Audio:** mass BGM for the unboxing → comedy or tragedy for the verdict
- **Example:** a gimmicky USB gadget for the room setup, verdict included.
- ⚠️ If any brand ever pays or gifts you, use the **Paid partnership** label and **licensed music** ([15 §6](15-tamil-music-and-audio-strategy.md#6-licensing--legal-india-specific)).

### F6 · Build → Reveal on the Drop *(P3 · 15–30 s)*
- **Structure:** hook shows the final result for 0.5 s → "Built this from ___" → fast beat-synced timelapse → **reveal exactly on the musical drop** → one funny final beat (the mess, the injury, the rabbit sitting on it)
- **Audio:** a trending or motivational Tamil track with a clear drop
- **Example:** room LED setup glow-up, ending with the rabbit chewing the wire.

### F7 · How I Made It (educational) *(P3 · 30–45 s)*
- **Structure:** "You asked how I made this" (reply-to-comment sticker) → 3–5 steps with on-screen numbers → the mistake I made → result
- **Audio:** light BGM under your voiceover (music ducked 18–25 dB, [06 §6](06-audio-and-sound-design.md#6-mixing-levels-for-reels))
- **Rule:** only after a build Reel gets "how?" comments ([14 §5](14-creator-identity-and-content-pillars.md#5-balancing-entertainment-lifestyle-and-education))

### F8 · Road Diaries *(P4 · 12–30 s)*
- **Structure:** golden-hour establishing shot → smooth rolling shots and POV → one relatable or funny text line (*"Petrol price paathu tears 🥲"*) → loop back to the opening shot
- **Audio:** nostalgic melody (the "Feel" 20%), or a mass BGM for bike shots
- **Signatures:** S5 MovieMode grade, S6 text

### F9 · POV Relatable *(P1 · 6–15 s)*
- **Structure:** "POV: ___" text on frame 1 → acted situation → reaction punch
- **Audio:** comedy dialogue or a trending audio
- **Example:** *"POV: Amma sees the 4th Amazon parcel this week"*

### F10 · Trend Remix *(Wildcard · trend length)*
- **Structure:** a trending format (Tamil or English), relocated into your world: pets, R15, room, Verna
- **Rule:** must contain your twist plus at least 2 signatures. Run it as a **Trial Reel** first ✅ ([02 §5](02-reels-growth-strategy.md#5-testing-system-trial-reels--iteration))

### F11 · Character Cross-Over *(P1/P2/P4 · 15–25 s, occasional)*
- **Structure:** two "characters" from different pillars meet (rabbit vs R15 helmet, birds "judging" your DIY)
- **Why:** ties the multi-interest world together into one "cast", so followers of one pillar meet the others

## 5. Storytelling formats

| Format | Structure | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Setup → Escalation → Bruh** | Normal situation → it gets more dramatic → absurd fail | F1, F2, F9 |
| **Movie in 15 seconds** | Intro (name card) → conflict → climax → twist | F4, F11 |
| **ABT** (And, But, Therefore) | "I wanted X, BUT Y happened, THEREFORE Z (stupid solution)" | F2, F5, F6 |
| **Loop ending** | The last shot matches the first, so it replays seamlessly | F8, F9 |
| **Callback** | Reference an earlier Reel's running joke (the rabbit's villain theme returns) | Series |

## 6. Pacing guidance for your style

| Section | Pace |
|---|---|
| Cinematic or dramatic part | **Slower, deliberate:** slow-mo, longer holds (1–2 s), building music |
| The switch / bruh moment | **Instant:** a hard cut on the exact frame |
| Comedy reality part | **Fast:** jump cuts, punch-ins every 1–2 s |
| Emotional (Road Diaries) | Smooth 1.5–3 s shots cut on the beat |

🔵 **The contrast in pace *is* the joke.** Don't rush the dramatic setup, or the switch won't land.

- [ ] One pillar only. Episode tag correct
- [ ] Captions in the house style, Tanglish, proofread
- [ ] Cover follows the template
- [ ] Feels like *me*, not a generic influencer edit
